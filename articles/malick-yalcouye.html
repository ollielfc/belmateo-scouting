Player Analysis: Malick Yalcouye

Birthdate: November 18, 2005
Foot: Right
Height: 5'7
Nationality: Mali / Ivory Coast
Contract Expiry: December 31, 2028

In April of 2024, Allsvenskan (Sweden’s first division) talent Lucas Bergvall joined Tottenham for an estimated €10 million. Many football fans in Sweden considered him to be the most promising player coming from the league in years and were confident of his ability to become a European star.

Just two months later in June, Allsvenskan fans already had their eyes on a player who some believe to be even more talented… Malick Yalcouye, an 18-year-old who was born in a small town in Mali.

Yalcouye, who a year ago was playing for ASEC Mimosas in the Ivorian Premier Division, is now regularly starting for one of Sweden’s most historically successful clubs, IFK Goteborg. After being in Europe for only a few months, he has already attracted interest from some of the biggest names in the sport (Liverpool, Manchester United, Bayern, Brighton).

The question being asked now is, what makes him so good?

Defending:

Although possessing a fair amount of diversity in his positions, Yalcouye is most often played as an 8. When you look at his heatmap, it becomes clear that no matter his position, he will still be in and around the ball.

This is mainly credited to his amazing stamina, as he can run all game and come out looking like he can go again.

Being 5’7, Malick doesn’t necessarily have the long legs that most players in his position have. This is something that he perfectly counters, by being extremely intelligent and well-timed and positioned when attempting to take the ball. With this, he has a rapid turn of pace and acceleration that lets him reach the ball without needing to completely stretch himself and risk being dribbled around or left out of position.

As you can see, he times his pounces on the ball exceptionally well.

Another key trait of Yalcouye is his aggression to win the ball. Partly due to his age, and partly due to his size, Malick does not possess much natural strength or weight. However, this does nothing to stop him from putting his body onto opponents in an attempt to win the ball. He very clearly has no fear when going into tackles, and will put his back, shoulder, or hip to gain the advantage on an opponent (this contributes to why he averages 1.5 fouls p/90).

Although not carrying it now, it’s clear that he can still grow into his body and become a massive annoyance to any attackers trying to out-muscle him.

Attacking:

As mentioned previously, Yalcouye’s height means he doesn’t have quite the reach with his legs. However, he uses this to his strength, as his short stride allows him to successfully control the ball straight from the tackle. Pair this with his amazing endurance, and you have a player who can create instant counterattacks in the final third.

Not only can he win the ball, but he is more than capable of carrying and progressing the ball up the pitch. He is also skilled at receiving the ball and using tight turns and control to break away from defenders.

His pace certainly helps, but his short center of gravity plays a key role in his technique and ability with the ball at his feet. In addition to these traits, he includes small skills like body feints and stepovers when on the ball (but he usually keeps it simple).

Attributed to his dribbling prowess, he has won by far the most fouls in the Allsvenskan with 54. This means he averages about 5 fouls drawn p/90. There are a few reasons why he draws so many fouls, one being his previously mentioned small frame and low weight which allows opponents to knock him down fairly easily. Another being his quick acceleration which leads opponents to take a stab at the ball and catch his legs/feet instead.

Despite not being a part of his main strengths, Yalcouye possesses sound creative and finishing capabilities. In the current 2024 season, he has averaged 1.1 shots p/90, which can be improved upon. As for his passing, he averaged 1.5 key passes p/90, which is fairly decent, and around 40 passes p/90. Most of his passes are simple and allow other players on his team to contribute to the play. It’s clear he is restricted with his shooting and passing when it comes to his role in the team, and it’s yet to be known if he can excel in these departments too.

Tactical Responsibility and Where He Could Play Next

Similar to Manu Koné, who I also have written about, Yalcouye could play in pretty much any midfield role. He definitely plays the enabler role on his team, which is extremely underrated in the grand scheme of the sport.

With his current array of expertise, the best position for him now is the 8. This mainly comes down to his admirable stamina, and ability to help his team in many ways. A high-pressing team could take advantage of his engine, and use him as a way to win the ball in the opponent's half. A counter-attacking team could greatly benefit from his skillset, as he can win the ball and quickly transition the team into a goal-scoring opportunity. A team with possession could use his box-crashing and adept take-ons to help create attacking chances.

Generally speaking, Malick could be useful for any team within his current capabilities. As of today, he could be useful to teams in the Championship, Ligue 1, Eredivisie, Liga Portugal, and so on.

With more than enough room to grow, he undoubtedly has the potential to play for a Champions League-level team. It would not be surprising to have people jealous that their team doesn’t have such an intelligent and hard-working athlete. A great way to summarize Yalcouye is with this cliche but effective quote from a commentator during his performance against Mjällby AIF this season:

“The world is 70% water, the rest is covered by Yalcouyé.”
